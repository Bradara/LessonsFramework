# The Biggest Edge in AI Era is... Learning How to Learn

Your brain is a miracle machine. It fires a quadrillion signals every second. By the time you end up reading this sentence, it would have fired several quadrillion signals in your brain. 

Impressive. Until you meet GPT-5. 

If your brain had to match GPT-5's single training run, it would have to run for 30,000 years. We were in the ice age 30,000 years ago! 

AI is already operating on a scale that we can't comprehend anymore.

ONE INSIGHT

Let’s try a quick experiment. I want you to look at these numbers and memorize them just by looking at them just once: 0712196708121967. 

Did you get it? Some of you will, others may have a hard time. When your brain sees some random numbers, it gets overwhelmed trying to keep track of independent ideas. It’s too much. 

But what if we turned these numbers into a single idea? First, the first eight numbers are the Pearl Harbor Day. The next date is the day after. (Sorry, I'm writing the dates in American format, so mm/dd/yyyy… I have become such a New Yorker!) 

This is called chunking, and it’s your brain's compression algorithm. It exists for two reasons: evolution designed the brain to process threads faster. A tiger coming out of the bushes required quick chunking. 

But evolution had not taken into account that the same human brain will someday give rise to AI. There is a study in 2024 done by Stanford and MIT on AI's impact, and the study found that consultants were able to finish their projects 25% faster using AI, but their work was 40% less original. So yes, faster, but formulaic. Original thinking goes out the window. This guarantees intellectual atrophy. Because there is no struggle. 

The key insight is the crucial anecdote to today’s AI struggle: use AI as a coach, not as a crutch. Increase the quality and quantity of their struggle, not decrease it. 

ONE TOOL

Your brain doesn't hate struggle. It hungers for it. I’ve spoken about the 3C protocol in [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=npQ2IORdlvU&utm_campaign=the-biggest-edge-in-ai-era-is-learning-how-to-learn&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=sandeepswadia.beehiiv.com) that helps us learn better faster in the age of AI. The one tool I’ll share here is one of the three Cs. Compress.

**Compression is about** cutting ruthlessly. Recent research shows that the brain can only juggle 4+1 independent ideas at a time. Three steps that helps select, compress, and chunk: 

*   Selection: what's the 20% that gives 80% of the value? Focus on learning that. This is a great area to get AI to be your ally. If you want to read a book, ask which areas you should focus on. Let AI take your interview and suggest the most efficient way to learn the most in the least amount of time.
    
*   Association: how does this connect to something I already know? Anytime you connect the new information with what you already know, the retention gets stronger. Analogies and visualization also help. When I was in business school, I had trouble remembering the concept of opportunity costs. So I thought of ordering food from a menu. Every time you order one dish, your opportunity cost is the next best dish you passed up. Was it perfect? Not at all. But it worked for me. You can find your own hacks. 
    
*   Chunking: compress it into one simple model, a metaphor, a drawing, a phrase you'll remember. Cognitive studies on chess grandmasters estimate that they can internalize 50,000 or even 100,000 patterns on the chessboard. But they're not memorizing. They compress what they have learned into patterns that their brain can actually handle. 
    

ONE PRACTICE

I’ve always struggled with learning. I get bored very easily. I might even be flirting with ADHD, but I've never bothered to check. 

One key practice that I have learned the hard way is to get out of my head. When I am learning, I remember that I cannot be the performer and the critic at the same time. While you’re learning, be the performer. Shut down that voice of your inner critic. That way, when you face difficulties grasping a concept, you at least don't make it harder by kicking yourself for not being as smart as somebody else. There'll always be someone better, smarter, faster than you. So what? Who cares? They can't be you! 

Your self-trust is your biggest weapon in your ability to learn. 

We’re heading to a world where AI will pass the bar exam and help oncologists diagnose cancer. In that world, learning how to learn fast will be our only renewable resource.
